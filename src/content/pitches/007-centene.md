# Centene (CNC)

These are my personal draft notes for the Centene pitch. The aim is to turn the raw thesis into a clearer article covering the business, valuation, upside, risks, and what I learned.

## Valuation

Steady State Earnings of $3.5 - 4.5 Bn

Implied multiple today is ~3.5x

Historical average multiple of 10 - 12x

Implied valuation of $40 - 45 Bn

Upside of 150% (Options 400%)

## Key business lines

Marketplace (ACA): Struggling with high MLRs due to higher morbidity rates due to “healthy members” leaving the marketplace as a result of integrity measures. Integrity measures are likely to continue in 2026. Pressure on membership count will continue at least till 2026 but repricing should start to show some effects and margins should at least partially recover. There is also the very real possibility of removal of eAPTCs in 2026 which can result in further decrease to memberships. Key data points as of Q2 2025.

5.9 mm members

More than 10 bn in premiums in Q2 alone

Earnings impact of $2.4 bn for 100% members in FY 2025

Medicaid: Very high MLR of 94.9% due to (i) behavioral health (ii) home health and (iii) high cost drugs. Florida was a key culprit in behavioral and NY was the key culprit in home health. Both can be managed and 100% of book should be repriced next year. Expect at least some margin recovery.

Medicare: Good membership growth over the last few years but targeting positive contribution in 2027

## Investment Thesis

Medicaid and ACA have very high regulatory risks but threshold for pain in the US population is very very low and that should play out in midterms in 2026 with lower than expected impact to Medicaid and ACA. Both programs (and medicare) are undergoing a shakeout with redeterminations and integrity measures but have a very long history of growth at this point

Scale advantages are real in health insurance - UNH, CI, Aetna, BCBS etc have been at the top of the industry for a long long time. HUM and CNC are more recent due to very large focus on govt programs

Expecting lower competition as competitors focus on profitability of their own books given industry wide underperformance over the last two years and higher MLRs

Even assuming a 25% haircut to memberships but with a 10% increase to revenue per member through increase in premiums, revenues ($160 Bn) may go down by up to 15% ($136 Bn; downside). Assuming Centene can get back to 3.5% margins (in line with pre C19) in 2 years ($4.5 Bn operating earnings), and lets net out about $1 Bn in other expenses including interest and taxes ($3.5 Bn). That should get us to the desired 150% upside. Finally, they have ~$5 Bn in tangible book value (cash/investments) if they stop operations today and distribute to shareholders. That brings effective EV to ~10Bn today on ~4Bn earnings power

## Risks

Can lose market share to competitors as Centene looks to reprice offerings. However, most if not all of the industry is undergoing similar issues so it will be surprising (but possible)

Management does not have much “skin in the game”. CEO seems relatively new and untested. Management may therefore be over optimistic in their ability to reprice

Bill Nygren and David Einhorn are investors but is small sizes - surprising when UNH has a long list of superinvestors including Berkshire
