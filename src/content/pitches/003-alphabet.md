# Alphabet (GOOG)

These are my personal draft notes for the Alphabet pitch. The aim is to turn the raw thesis into a clearer article covering the business, valuation, upside, risks, and what I learned.

## Valuation

- Revenue CAGR: 18.16%

- EPS Growth (5Y): 21%

- $110B in cash, negligible debt

- Free Cash Flow: Strong, but 32% of FCF in 2024 went to stock-based comp (SBC)

- Share Buybacks: 13.7B shares (2016) → 12.6B today (~8% reduction)

- Multiples (NTM):

P/E: 17.5x

EV/Revenue: 4.77x

EV/EBITDA: 10.75x

MC/FCF: 25.2x (40x ex-SBC adjustment)

## Key business lines

Search & Ads (~80% of revenue): 90%+ global market share, monetizing intent-driven queries (“best car insurance,” “divorce lawyer near me”) with unmatched ROI for advertisers.People pay billions to google for SEO.

YouTube: Nearly Netflix-sized revenue; subscriptions (Premium) remain under-monetized relative to engagement levels.

Google Cloud: 29% YoY growth; gaining ground on AWS/Azure with differentiated AI infrastructure (e.g., TPUs).

Waymo: 100k+ paid rides weekly across three cities; early but real revenue traction in autonomous mobility.

Ecosystem Flywheel: Chrome, Android, Maps, Gmail, and DeepMind AI all reinforce distribution, data capture, and monetization.

## Investment Thesis

Alphabet is fundamentally an advertising business with a global software empire wrapped around it. Ads turn intent into dollars at scale. a model hard to disrupt.

Search dominance is self-reinforcing: best data → better algorithms → higher conversions → more ad spend.

Growth adjacencies (YouTube, Cloud, Waymo, AI) expand revenue base beyond ads, creating “multiple shots on goal.”

AI risk is overstated:

While ChatGPT and generative AI hype spooked markets, Google has been training superior models for years (Gemini 2.5 now state-of-the-art).

Distribution moat (Chrome + Android + Gmail) ensures reach to billions ,something OpenAI or competitors can’t match (they would need 3rd party and hence capex and $$$)

Monetization optionality: Many widely used services (Maps, Gmail, Drive) remain lightly monetized; turning them into cash machines is a lever Google hasn’t fully pulled yet.

Capital return discipline: Buybacks have steadily shrunk share count despite SBC drag.(but google does not have a good history of capital returns as they always invest heavily in capex)

## Risks

AI Capex: Heavy infra investments dent short-term FCF, with uncertain near-term payoff.

Cloud Competition: AWS and Azure still lead in enterprise adoption.

Regulatory: Antitrust suits and privacy legislation are recurring risks.

Content Competition: YouTube faces pressure from Netflix, Prime, TikTok, though ad model and scale provide resilience.

Management SBC: High stock-based comp dilutes FCF efficiency.

## Why Now?

Market is overly focused on “AI disruption” (ChatGPT killing Google narrative). In reality, Alphabet’s data + distribution moat makes it extremely difficult to dislodge.

At ~17.5x earnings(less than the S&P), Alphabet trades at a reasonable multiple for a business growing ~20% annually with massive FCF.

Cloud and AI infrastructure spending creates near-term pressure but lays groundwork for future dominance.

Buybacks + cash hoard ($110B) offer downside support.

## Upside Math

Base Case: EPS continues compounding at ~10% annually; at a 17–18x multiple, you get steady 10–12% CAGR returns.

Re-rating Case: If EPS keeps growing and the market re-rates Google closer to 22–25x (its historical premium), upside expands  in longer term

Optionality kicker: Waymo autonomy scale-up or YouTube subscription monetization could add $50–100B incremental valuation over time.
