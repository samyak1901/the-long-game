# Cigna (CI)

Cigna is a note on healthcare scale, PBM risk, multiple compression, and whether a painful transition can make future cash flows more understandable rather than less.

## Valuation

FY25E EPS: $29.6 → FY26E EPS: ~$30

Current P/E: ~7.8x

Implied value: $360–$390

Upside: ~60% ( in 2 years)

## Key business line

3 businesses.

Health insurer (~40% of biz),

Specialty & Care (~30%)

and PBM (~30%).

Company has historically traded at a discount to peers because of its PBM segment.

## Thesis

PBM segment's regulatory risk has been a drag on the company's valuation.

Despite growing EPS at a 13% CAGR over 10 yrs, it has traded <10x P/E from time to time. We can buy today  at 7.8x 2025E EPS.

Along with 3Q earnings, mgmt announced a change in PBM business model. Over the next couple of yrs, it will do away with the much-maligned drug rebate model. Soon, customers will get a straight discount at the counter when buying drugs. Company will collect a fee.

Implementing this new model will require heavy investment in 2026/27. In addition, they extended the contract of 3 largest PBM customers to 2030 at a lower margin. Combination of these 2 things will result in decline in PBM profitability in '26.

However, overall earnings will grow yoy in '26 because the remaining 70% of the business (health insurer & specialty care) will more than offset PBM decline.

2025E EPS guide is >$29.60 so 2026 might be ~$30. Growth should accelerate in '27 and return to 10+% by '28.

So pain for 2 years before getting back to its normal growth trajectory. But I believe the story is even better than that. PBM's revamped biz model, along with contract extension for 3 largest customers, significantly de-risks PBM's cashflow.

This is especially true since they expect that PBM's margin will be consistent with the past (~4% pre-tax) after investment-heavy 2026/27. So whatever low multiple investors have applied to their PBM in the past due reg risk, it should be far higher.
