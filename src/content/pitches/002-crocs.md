# Crocs (CROX)

Crocs is a question of perception versus economics: a brand the market doubts, but a business with unusual margins, cash generation, and buyback capacity.

ValuationCurrent Price: $97.98 (Nov 2024)Forward P/E: ~7.3x (vs industry 15–18x) → clear relative undervaluationEV/EBITDA: ~7x (vs comps 10–12x)Fair Value (Relative): $130–160 per share5-Year Target (Base Case): $180–200/shareAssumptions: 4–5% revenue CAGR, stable margins, EPS growth 5–7%Implied return: 13–15% CAGR over 5 yearsPEG Ratio: ~0.5x (EPS growth vs P/E multiple), suggests undervaluation even with modest growth

## Key business lines

Core Footwear (Crocs): Crocs’ signature line and Crocs Brand continues to anchor revenue and brand identity.

HeyDude Acquisition: Expanding casual footwear portfolio, though margins are thinner and integration is ongoing.

Global Expansion: Growth opportunity in Asia and Europe, though brand strength is U.S.-heavy.

Direct-to-Consumer (DTC): Increasing online + retail mix, supporting margins but exposed to consumer sentiment swings.

## Investment Thesis

Crocs trades at deep discount multiples vs peers despite steady growth.Brand strength + operating efficiency support resilience even in discretionary downturns.

HeyDude integration + international expansion provide medium-term growth levers.

Even assuming modest growth, Crocs’ valuation leaves room for multiple expansion (7x → 10–12x EV/EBITDA).

Upside case: if Crocs sustains ~7% EPS growth and re-rates to peer multiples, stock could double within 5 years.

No other footwear comes close to Crocs op margins (it's now more than LVMH, let that sink in), it earns around 1B in earnings/cf on 4B revenue.

Buybacks auth is of around 1.3b , which is around 20-25% of the company

## Risks

Consumer Sentiment: As a discretionary brand, sales are cyclical and vulnerable to recessions.

Competition: Nike, Deckers, VF, and others squeeze margins; fashion risk if Crocs’ style loses relevance.

Margin Pressure: Signs of compression already visible; sustained pressure could cap upside.

Market does not like Crocs in general, it trades like a bankrupt company ( however this is not a risk per se, but the perception needs to change for it to produce shareholder returns)

## Why Now?

At ~7x earnings, Crocs is priced like a declining brand, but fundamentals show continued growth.

Market is overly focused on fashion risk + margin compression, overlooking structural profitability and brand stickiness.

Relative valuation gap to peers is too wide to persist if Crocs delivers on 5%+ growth.Provides asymmetric risk/reward: downside supported by low multiple, upside driven by re-rating + moderate growth.
