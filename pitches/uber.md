# Uber (UBER)

Uber is a platform thesis: mobility, delivery, subscriptions, ads, and autonomous risk all meeting in one question about whether demand aggregation is the durable layer.

## Valuation

- In 2026: 12 Bn in FCF, 8 Bn in adj. FCF.
- 2024 FCF of 6.5 Bn, growing at 40% CAGR.
- Includes $2bn of SBC (which should not grow) and $2bn of insurance reserves (which will grow).
- Current market cap is ~160 Bn (5% 2026 FCF yield), with expected growth of mid-teens at the minimum going forward. Current revenue CAGRs are 20+%.
- Total 2026 yield is likely >20% on current prices.
- Management has full control over achieving 2026 targets, driven by margin expansion.
- For a platform like Uber, you would place at least a 30x multiple on adj. FCF in 2026, which gets you to a ~$250 Bn market cap. This is 50% growth in 2 years on a trailing basis. Share-price target of 115 in 2026 at least.

## Three key questions

### 1. Does Uber have runway for growth? YES

- They are getting increasingly dominant in the US. The Uber One subscription is the best value among delivery services by far.
- Most competitors are unprofitable and will find it increasingly difficult to continue competing against Uber.
- Macro tailwinds as car-ownership prices continue to increase and Uber provides increasingly better value.
- May not be in the 20%+ range, but mid-teens is reasonable.
- They keep experimenting, in the US, you can use a courier service, reserve rental cars, get groceries (basically everything) on top of Eats (with ads) and mobility. Also partnered with taxis in certain locations.
- The power of the platform is underestimated.

### 2. Will management be shareholder friendly? Tentative YES

- CEO comes from Expedia, a more mature company.
- CFO appears shareholder-focused.
- Pulled out of an autonomous-driving startup in 2020 and are making smaller partnership investments that are better than large transformative acquisitions. Management is clear in recent interviews that autonomous tech will be a commodity and they will focus on providing value through their platform. Makes a ton of sense to me.
- Still questions over very high SBC (management guided to using 6 Bn of buybacks to offset SBC, so share count should be stable or declining going forward); random equity stakes (management guided to reducing these over time); and clever reporting (which I suppose comes with the territory of venture-backed companies).
- CEO owns 4+ million shares (and options) as of 2024, up from about 1.4 mm in 2022. He has subsequently sold well over 1 mm shares, of which at least 750k were options close to expiration. We will learn more in the 2025 proxy soon.

### 3. With the advent of self-driving, will Uber become redundant? Is there a binary risk? NO

- Most tech becomes commoditized sooner rather than later, note the news about the use of synthetic data to train self-driving.
- The transition will be slow because many, many vehicles are already on the road.
- A multimodal world, with robots for neighbourhood deliveries in cities (SF pilot) and drones for suburbs (China already cracked this), will all need a place to find demand. And Uber is the funnel for all delivery-related demand.
